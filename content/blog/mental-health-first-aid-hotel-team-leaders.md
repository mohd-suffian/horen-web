Hotel work asks people to stay warm and composed for the full length of a shift, whatever is happening in their own lives. Long hours, rotating rosters, peak seasons and difficult guests all add up. Most of the strain stays invisible until it shows up as a resignation letter, a string of sick days or a guest complaint that started with a tired, overwhelmed team member.

October is Mental Health Awareness Month, which makes it a good time to ask a practical question: what can a supervisor actually do?

## A team leader is not a therapist

The first thing to be clear about is the limit of the role. A supervisor does not diagnose, counsel or fix. Their job is to notice early, respond with care and connect the person to the right help. That is what mental health first aid means in a workplace, and it is a skill that can be learned like any other.

We teach it in three steps: spot, support, refer.

## 1. Spot

Changes in behaviour are usually the first sign, and supervisors see their team more often than anyone. Watch for patterns that last more than a few days:

- Arriving late or calling in sick more often than usual
- Withdrawing from colleagues, skipping breaks with the team
- Irritability or short temper with guests or co-workers
- More mistakes in tasks they normally handle well
- Visible tiredness, or comments like "I can't keep up" or "what's the point"

One bad day is not a pattern. A change that persists is worth a conversation.

## 2. Support

Choose a private moment, not the middle of a busy check-in. Then keep it simple:

- **Open gently.** "I've noticed you seem a bit stretched lately. How are you doing?"
- **Listen more than you talk.** Resist the urge to give advice or share your own story.
- **Don't judge or diagnose.** "You're probably just stressed" closes the conversation. "That sounds like a lot to carry" keeps it open.
- **Ask what would help.** Sometimes it is a roster change or a lighter section for a week. Sometimes it is just being heard.

Respect confidentiality. Agree with the person what, if anything, will be shared and with whom.

## 3. Refer

Know where help sits before you need it:

- Your company's HR team or Employee Assistance Programme (EAP), if you have one
- A doctor or mental health professional
- Local support lines, such as Befrienders Kuala Lumpur (03-7627 2929), which offers confidential emotional support

If someone is at immediate risk of harming themselves or others, do not wait. Stay with them and call emergency services on 999.

## Why this matters for guests too

A team that feels safe to speak up is a team that recovers faster from a bad shift, supports each other during peak periods and stays longer. Guests feel the difference. Healthier people, happier guests.
