Most hotel teams have heard that AI will change hospitality. Fewer have a clear idea of what to do with it on Monday morning. The good news is that you don't need a big system or a large budget to start. The best first uses of AI are small, practical and close to work your team already does.

## Start with tasks, not tools

Ask each department one question: what do you write, summarise or rework over and over? That list is your starting point. Common answers include:

- **Replying to online reviews.** AI can draft a polite, personalised response that a manager then edits and approves.
- **Guest messages in other languages.** Quick translation for pre-arrival emails or WhatsApp enquiries, checked by someone before sending.
- **Shift handover notes.** Turning rough notes into a clear summary for the next shift.
- **SOP drafts and checklists.** A first draft of a new procedure, refined by the people who will use it.
- **Training material.** Quiz questions and role-play scenarios built from your own service standards.
- **Excel work.** Help writing formulas for rosters, payroll checks or occupancy reports.

None of these replace staff. They remove the blank-page problem and give time back for guests.

## Put guardrails in place first

AI is useful. It also makes confident mistakes, and it can create real risk if used carelessly. Agree a few rules before anyone starts:

**Never paste guest personal data into public AI tools.** Names, IC or passport numbers, contact details and card information stay out. This is a PDPA issue as well as a trust issue.

**A human checks everything that leaves the building.** Review replies, translations and anything a guest will read.

**Keep your brand voice.** Give the tool examples of how your property writes, and edit the output to match.

**Be careful with facts.** AI can invent policies, prices or details that sound right. Always check against your own information.

## A simple 30-day plan

**Week 1:** Pick one department and two tasks from the list above.

**Week 2:** Agree the guardrails and write two or three standard prompts the team can reuse.

**Week 3:** Use them daily. Note what saves time and what needs heavy editing.

**Week 4:** Share results with other departments and choose the next tasks.

Small wins build confidence faster than a big rollout.
